# Methodology

The library uses an eight-stage learning loop:

**Discover → Understand/Map → Baseline → Decompose → Position → Demonstrate → Prove → Expand**

It is a sales proof method, not a delivery lifecycle. A seller or SA may move backward whenever evidence changes the understanding of the work.

## Operating principles

1. Start with a mission or service outcome, not an automation feature.
2. Follow the work across teams, tools, approvals, waits, failures, and evidence.
3. Separate facts from assumptions and illustrative examples.
4. Baseline before proposing improvement.
5. Preserve human judgment where policy, risk, or accountability demands it.
6. Reuse governed capabilities; do not hide duplication inside use cases.
7. A successful job is not sufficient proof. Verify service state and business acceptance.
8. Qualify expansion with measured local evidence and maintenance cost.

## Stage contract

| Stage | Primary question | Required output | Exit signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | What outcome matters, to whom, and why now? | Outcome card, stakeholders, scope, hypotheses | Sponsor agrees on problem and decision |
| Understand/Map | How does the work actually flow? | SOP, current-state map, systems, decisions, exceptions | People who do the work recognize the map |
| Baseline | What happens now and how will it be measured? | MBPM worksheet, sources, sample window | Metrics have definitions, owners, and obtainable data |
| Decompose | What is judgment, existing automation, reusable work, and gap? | Step classification and capability map | Boundaries and missing contracts are explicit |
| Position | Where does AAP add coordination value? | Solution pattern, product roles, non-goals | Native tool ownership and AAP role are credible |
| Demonstrate | Can the proposed workflow be experienced safely? | Repeatable demo, talk track, expected results | Another SA can run and reset it |
| Prove | Did the agreed outcome improve in this environment? | POV plan, evidence, acceptance decision | Sponsor can make an evidence-based decision |
| Expand | What adjacent service is justified next? | Qualified expansion options and ownership | Expansion has a sponsor, hypothesis, and capacity |

## Evidence model

Every claim should be one of:

- **Observed fact** — supported by customer evidence and date.
- **Customer statement** — attributed to a role and validated if material.
- **Hypothesis** — testable, with a planned observation.
- **Illustrative example** — invented solely to explain the pattern.
- **External evidence** — cited, bounded, and not substituted for local proof.

Proof links four things:

~~~text
baseline metric -> intervention -> observed result -> expansion decision
~~~

## Why the sequence matters

The Denver methodology makes organizational knowledge explicit before automation: understand the work, document the SOP, decompose it, automate the deterministic parts, and remeasure. The lifecycle deck adds governed orchestration, verification, evidence, failure paths, and the Baseline-to-Proof-to-Qualified-Expansion decision. The platform operating model adds product ownership, reusable services, adoption, and ReCommoning.

Together they prevent two common failures: a demo with no customer outcome, and a one-off playbook with no durable owner.
